A zero-dependency Node.js CLI that tracks your climb up the fifteen-step ladder in the post
[15 Steps to Learn AI GTM](https://www.onegtmlab.dev/blog/15-steps-to-learn-ai-gtm).
The post's argument: a lot of people start at step twelve, wanting agents and orchestration, and skip the ladder that makes them
work, so an agent inherits every weakness underneath it. The order is fixed: foundations first, engineering second, agents last.
The ladder is not a difficulty ranking, it is a **dependency chain**.

## The ladder

| Level | Steps |
| --- | --- |
| 1. AI-assisted | 1 GTM foundations, 2 Prompt like an operator, 3 Sharpen your ICP, 4 Positioning and messaging, 5 Content leverage |
| 2. GTM engineering | 6 Signals over lists, 7 Data and enrichment, 8 Signal pipelines, 9 Timing and relevance, 10 Outbound systems |
| 3. Agentic GTM | 11 Operator setup, 12 Research to outreach, 13 Orchestration, 14 Measurement loops, 15 Compounding engine |

## Use

Requires Node.js 18+.

```bash
node bin/ladder.js list                 # the 15 steps by level
node bin/ladder.js show 12              # what a step means and which earlier steps it draws on
node bin/ladder.js done 1 2 3           # mark steps you can honestly call solid
node bin/ladder.js status               # checklist, level progress, gaps, next step
node bin/ladder.js next                 # the one step to work on now
node bin/ladder.js tools 2              # established vs still-moving tools for a level
```

```
level 1: 3/5, level 2: 0/5, level 3: 0/5
next: step 4, Positioning and messaging
```

State lives in `.ai-gtm-ladder.json` in the current folder (`--file PATH` to change it).

How it follows the post:

- **Where to start**: `next` implements "find the highest step you can honestly say is solid, then start at the one after it".
- **Dependency chain**: `status` lists any unsolid step *below* your highest solid one as a gap, because teams fail by trying to
  buy step 15 without building steps 1 through 10.
- **Named dependencies**: where the post says a step uses earlier ones (step 9 reuses step 5's content; step 10 is possible
  because of steps 6 to 9; step 12 uses the ICP, messaging, signals and enrichment of steps 3, 4, 6 and 7; step 15 is the
  residue of step 14), `status` warns when a solid step draws on one that is not solid yet.
- **Tools**: `tools` separates *established* (stable enough to build a process around) from *still moving* (worth using and
  worth watching), per level, as the post does.

Nothing is scored automatically: you decide when a step is solid.
